# Relatives discovery - 2026-11-11: evidence register

Status: RESEARCH EVIDENCE ONLY. Nothing here was built or decided. Every claim below is a research agent's reading of a public source; treat it as a lead to check, not a ruling. All sources are public (T0). No real-person data appears here; scholars and officials are named only as authors or as office holders named in their own publications.

## How to read this

- **What produced it.** A 24-agent discovery workflow run on 2026-11-11: five code and plan audits, eight web research topics (R1-R8 below), six ideation lenses, three judges, and two Jev citation-check batches. This register holds the research and verification layers only; the audits, ideas and judge scores are not reproduced here.
- **Finding ids.** `R<topic>-F<n>`. Findings marked **load-bearing** are the ones the research agents said a design would lean on. For those, the supporting quote was fetched from the source and checked.
- **Quotes.** Quotes are as fetched, except that every em dash is rendered as a spaced hyphen, including inside quotes (I10). En dashes in ranges are rendered as plain hyphens.
- **Corrections.** Four claims were corrected after the Jev check (R2-F4, R3-F10, R5-F11, R7-F4). The topic sections show the corrected text, marked *(corrected)*; the Corrections section gives the original and why.
- **What Jev's scores mean.** Each compound claim was split into one-fact probes, each scored against a tight passage copied from the fetched page; the claim's score is the minimum across its probes. A score answers only: does this passage state this fact. Controls in both batches separated supported (0.95-0.99) from unsupported or contradicted (0.02-0.03) cleanly.
- **What they do not mean.** The controls were easy, so trust scores in the 0.4-0.8 band less. The checking agents chose the passages after reading the sources, so a high score mostly means the paraphrase matches the source, not that the source is authoritative, representative or independent. Verdicts and probabilities are the checking agents' judgment informed by Jev, not raw Jev output. Rely on the Noul 'supported' score, not Choice confidence (which was near 1.00 on everything). Some sources are vendor or blog claims (EmbeddingGemma, WebLLM, Willison, Verraes, iroh). Crate versions, IESG states and similar facts are point-in-time as of 2026-11-11; re-check before shipping.
- **Not checked by Jev.** Derived arithmetic (for example 'twelve years'), attributions and affiliations not in the passage (for example 'Inuit-led', 'Citizen Potawatomi'), the IP AI 'seven guidelines' count, and the GIDA publication year. Only public passage text and claim sentences were sent to Jev; no project data.

## R1 - Tribal climate coordination needs

Tribes and the assessments written about them describe the same coordination needs again and again. They lack steady climate staff. They spend too much effort answering many separate agencies. They lack local data, and they want to talk more with other Tribes. Federal reviews of 2025-2026 add that agency and Tribal staff are both too thin for shared decision-making. Meanwhile, the human connective tissue was cut. The NOAA-funded Northwest Climate Resilience Collaborative (NCRC), whose partners included ATNI, was terminated on May 5, 2025. The BIA/CASC Tribal Community Resilience Liaison network, whose staff worked as extension agents linking Tribal needs to resources, ended on July 17, 2026. Indian Affairs lost a net 11% of its staff. Roughly 45% of Northwest Tribes' FY2024 federal funding is at risk. Intertribal bodies answered with coalitions, but Tribal staff say they "don't know who to talk to." For Community Navigator, this is the strongest documented gap: a sovereignty-respecting directory of who is linked to what, and who can route a need to a resource, now that funded liaisons and agency staff are gone.

### Findings

- **R1-F1** - load-bearing. Northwest coastal Tribes, in listening sessions, named five barriers: staff capacity, funding, partnerships, technical assistance, and communication. They asked for agencies to coordinate so Tribes deal with fewer separate entities, and for better communication among the Tribes themselves.
  - Implication: The graph's first job is to reduce load: one Tribe-controlled profile of needs and capacities, reused across many agency and partner requests, instead of a fresh consultation every time. The same sessions asked for 'Effective communication among Northwest coastal Tribes around climate adaptation' and for consent protocols agreed in advance, which match peer edges and per-attribute sharing.
  - Source: Climate Adaptation Barriers and Needs Experienced by Northwest Coastal Tribes: Key Findings from Tribal Listening Sessions (NCRC / Climate Impacts Group / ATNI / WA Sea Grant) (2024-08 (file revised 2025-05-07)). <https://cig.uw.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/Climate-Adaptation-2-pager-FINAL_2025-05-07.pdf>
- **R1-F2** - load-bearing. The USGS/BIA Tribal Community Resilience Liaison network ended in July 2026 after twelve years. Its liaisons worked as extension agents, linking Tribal needs to resources.
  - Implication: Need-to-resource routing was the liaisons' job, and nothing now funds it. A routing graph that keeps their relationship knowledge in the hands of the Tribes and intertribal bodies that host it fills a real, newly opened gap. The liaison is the natural model for a bridge node that has consented to the role.
  - Source: Supporting Tribal Resilience - USGS Climate Adaptation Science Centers (2026 (page states the July 17, 2026 end date)). <https://www.usgs.gov/ecosystems/climate-adaptation-science-centers/tribal-resilience-liaisons>
- **R1-F3** - load-bearing. The Fifth National Climate Assessment finds that Indigenous Peoples often lack the staff to carry out climate plans. It also finds that funding comes with matching, reimbursement, and reporting burdens that Tribal administrative units cannot carry.
  - Implication: Capacity is the scarce resource, so the graph should model staff time, skills, and shareable services, such as a grant writer or GIS analyst a Tribe or consortium can lend. Then 'who can help' covers people and pooled capacity, not only organizations.
  - Source: Fifth National Climate Assessment, Chapter 16: Tribes and Indigenous Peoples (Capacity Infrastructure section) (2023). <https://toolkit.climate.gov/NCA5_Ch16_Tribes-and-Indigenous-Peoples>
- **R1-F4** - load-bearing. GAO found that federal agencies had not assessed their capacity for shared decision-making agreements with Tribes after the 2025 workforce cuts. Tribal officials told GAO they lacked staff to pursue such agreements and were often heavily outnumbered at meetings.
  - Implication: Co-stewardship is a pipeline that capacity limits. A graph that can show which Tribes, agencies, and units already have agreements, and who staffed them, lets a Tribe borrow templates and peer experience. That cuts the cost of each new agreement.
  - Source: GAO-26-106626, Shared decision-making agreements with Tribes (2026-01-28). <https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-106626/index.html>
- **R1-F5** - load-bearing. NOAA terminated the Northwest Climate Resilience Collaborative, effective May 5, 2025, along with the Tribal Stewards workforce program. The Collaborative's partners included ATNI, and it supported Tribes across Washington, Oregon, and Idaho.
  - Implication: Regional partnership webs funded by grants can vanish overnight, and their relationship maps and contacts go with them. An event-sourced graph held by the Tribes and intertribal bodies, not the grantee, keeps the network intact when funders leave.
  - Source: CIG Update: Termination of the Northwest Climate Resilience Collaborative (2025-05). <https://cig.uw.edu/2025/05/cig-update-termination-of-the-northwest-climate-resilience-collaborative>
- **R1-F6**. NCA5 says Indigenous Peoples lack environmental data for their own lands and waters and often get only data that are misaligned with their values. It also notes CARE principles are increasingly being applied in climate projects.
  - Implication: Links to datasets, observers, and monitoring capacity are themselves valuable things to route. TSDF tiers and CARE-aligned provenance on every edge are what make Tribes willing to share them at all.
  - Source: Fifth National Climate Assessment, Chapter 16 (Indigenous Data for Governance section) (2023). <https://toolkit.climate.gov/NCA5_Ch16_Tribes-and-Indigenous-Peoples>
- **R1-F7**. GAO reported that Indian Affairs' workforce fell by a net 11 percent between January and July 2025. Tribal leaders said staffing was already inadequate for trust responsibilities and service delivery was impaired.
  - Implication: Federal points of contact are churning. Agency-person edges in the graph need decay and review dates, so routing does not send Tribes to positions that no longer exist.
  - Source: GAO-26-108673, Indian Affairs workforce reductions (2026-01-27). <https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-108673/index.html>
- **R1-F8**. A Brookings conversation with NARF described a Coalition for Tribal Sovereignty of more than 40 Tribal-serving organizations formed in response to 2025 federal changes. It also described Tribes facing funding bottlenecks without knowing whom to contact.
  - Implication: 'Who do I talk to' is a pathfinding question, and the graph can answer it. The same piece warns that Tribal programs are 'losing critical institutional knowledge', which argues for capturing relationships as durable, Tribe-owned records.
  - Source: Robert Maxim and Allison Neswood examine the challenges Indian Country is facing due to the disruption in federal funding (Brookings) (2025-07-02). <https://www.brookings.edu/articles/robert-maxim-and-allison-neswood-examine-the-challenges-indian-country-is-facing-due-to-the-the-disruption-in-federal-funding/>
- **R1-F9**. A Portland State University analysis found that roughly $530 million of the $1.19 billion in federal funding for Northwest Tribes in FY2024 was at risk under 2025 legislation, including climate-resilience funding.
  - Implication: Funding is moving toward state, philanthropic, and Tribal-to-Tribal sources, such as Washington's Climate Commitment Act grants. The graph's 'resource' nodes should cover non-federal funders and in-kind help between Tribes, not only federal programs.
  - Source: Northwest Native Nations could lose hundreds of millions in federal funding, report says (InvestigateWest) (2025-09-24). <https://investigatewest.org/northwest-native-nations-could-lose-hundreds-of-millions-in-federal-funding-report-says/>
- **R1-F10**. Grist reported in November 2026 that DOGE-era cuts cost Tribes roughly $470 million, gutting climate resiliency plans, renewable energy, and jobs programs. Some grants, such as a Native Village of Kotzebue wind grant, remained paused.
  - Implication: Project status changes constantly, from active to frozen to terminated. Project and grant nodes need status as an attribute that changes over time, so Tribes can find others in the same limbo and share strategies.
  - Source: DOGE cuts cost tribes $470M, gutting funding for climate projects (Grist) (2026-11-02). <https://grist.org/indigenous/doge-cuts-cost-tribes-470-million-gutting-funding-for-climate-projects/>
- **R1-F11**. Federal auditors have warned for years that climate relocation projects need a lead agency to coordinate assistance. In 2025, funds for Newtok's move to Mertarvik were frozen even though dozens of grants from at least seven agencies had paid for it.
  - Implication: Relocations such as Newtok, Napakiak, and Quinault Taholah/Queets are multi-funder, multi-decade efforts with no coordinator. A graph view of each effort's funders, agencies, and peer relocating communities is a concrete product. The relocating communities are natural relatives to link.
  - Source: Forced to relocate by climate change, these Southwest Alaska villagers found a new crisis (Anchorage Daily News) (2025-05-29). <https://www.adn.com/alaska-news/rural-alaska/2025/05/29/forced-to-relocate-by-climate-change-these-southwest-alaska-villagers-found-a-new-crisis>
- **R1-F12**. During the November 2021 atmospheric-river floods, the Lummi levee failed and floodwater cut off every access road to the reservation. The Nation boated clinic staff in to keep the clinic running, and it declared an emergency again in December 2025.
  - Implication: In an emergency the useful edges are physical and local: boats, ports, clinics, and county-owned roads. Event-mode routing over pre-consented mutual-aid edges, such as who can lend a boat or staff a clinic, is a high-value, time-boxed use of broader sharing.
  - Source: Flooding is a familiar foe on the Lummi Reservation (KUOW) (2025-12-16). <https://www.kuow.org/environment/2025-12-16/flooding-is-a-familiar-foe-on-the-lummi-reservation>

### Exemplars

- **Tribal Community Resilience Liaison network (BIA TCR + USGS CASCs), 2014-2026.** Regional liaisons, often hosted by intertribal organizations such as ATNI, who connected Tribes with researchers, funders, and training.
  - Mechanism: Humans acting as extension agents: liaisons kept relationship knowledge in their heads and inboxes and matched Tribal needs to resources by hand. Funding ended July 17, 2026.
  - Lesson: This is the function Community Navigator can carry on. A liaison is a bridge node by consent, and the relationships it holds must outlive the liaison's funding, so they should sit in a graph the Tribes own.
  - <https://www.usgs.gov/ecosystems/climate-adaptation-science-centers/tribal-resilience-liaisons>
- **Pacific Northwest Tribal Climate Change Network (University of Oregon TCCP).** A long-running regional network (TCCP since 2009) of Tribal, agency, academic, and nonprofit participants. It exchanges information on climate policy, programs, and grants.
  - Mechanism: A mailing list and regular calls that broadcast opportunities. The network's input also shapes what research and resources the project produces.
  - Lesson: Broadcasting does not route: everyone receives everything. A permission-filtered graph can target an opportunity to the Tribes whose stated needs and capacities match it, and it can feed aggregate needs back into research agendas.
  - <https://tribalclimate.uoregon.edu/>
- **LEO Network (Local Environmental Observer), Alaska Native Tribal Health Consortium.** A platform started in 2012 where local observers post unusual environmental events. Topic experts are linked to those observations.
  - Mechanism: Geolocated observations from community members are connected to expert consultants, and a public map aggregates them across communities and regions.
  - Lesson: Pairing a local observation with an expert is a working, Tribally originated version of need-to-expertise routing. Community Navigator could treat observations as events that light up a routing path to people with the right expertise.
  - <https://www.cec.org/news/celebrated-citizen-science-platform-developed-in-arctic-expands-its-success-southward/>
- **Coalition for Tribal Sovereignty.** A coalition of more than 40 Tribal-serving organizations, including NARF, NCAI, and USET, formed in 2025 to defend trust-based programs.
  - Mechanism: Rapid alliance formation and coordinated letters and advocacy to the administration and Congress.
  - Lesson: Under shock, intertribal bodies form coalitions fast. A graph that already holds consented organization-to-organization edges, and shared exposure to the same terminated programs, can make that coalition discovery near-instant.
  - <https://www.brookings.edu/articles/robert-maxim-and-allison-neswood-examine-the-challenges-indian-country-is-facing-due-to-the-the-disruption-in-federal-funding/>
- **NCRC Tribal Coastal Resilience listening sessions.** Structured listening sessions with staff, citizens, and elected officials of Northwest coastal Tribes. They produced a shared map of barriers and needs.
  - Mechanism: Needs gathered across Tribes, combined into one regional set of findings, and published once for every partner to use.
  - Lesson: Pooled needs, published once, reduce consultation load. In the graph, Tribes can choose to publish needs at an aggregate (T0/T1) level, so funders and agencies query a single consented picture instead of interviewing each Tribe again.
  - <https://cig.uw.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/Climate-Adaptation-2-pager-FINAL_2025-05-07.pdf>
- **Washington Climate Commitment Act Tribal climate resilience funding (WA Commerce).** $52 million to 32 Tribes through direct appropriations, formula grants, and competitive grants. It included $13 million for Quinault's Taholah and Queets relocation infrastructure.
  - Mechanism: State non-competitive formula and direct funding alongside competitive grants.
  - Lesson: Funding that avoids competition eases the grant-writing burden and is growing in importance as federal money retreats. The resource layer of the graph should show state and formula channels next to federal ones.
  - <https://www.commerce.wa.gov/cca-tribal-climate-resilience/>

## R2 - Intertribal exemplars

The intertribal networks studied coordinate mainly through people: one or two named contacts per Nation, yearly in-person gatherings, cohort camps, and coordinators who route each request to the right expert. Their digital tools are thin and separate from each other. The clearest exception is SIKU, which lets each user set ownership, privacy and stewardship on every post and uses Indigenous terms in its species and ice profiles. Several networks are built around non-human relatives: First Foods, salmon managed "gravel-to-gravel", buffalo as "sacred beings", and seeds as "ancestors". Data sovereignty is usually handled through protocol rather than software: seed-keepers withhold variety names, Guardians programs use data-sharing agreements, and co-managers merge data into one shared reference. The gaps stated again and again are staff capacity, funding, red tape, systems that do not talk to each other, and reach (the burning network includes 12-26 of more than 500 Tribes). For Community Navigator this points to features built around a named contact per Nation, a broker who routes each need, per-item consent, and species or places as entities in the graph.

### Findings

- **R2-F1** - load-bearing. SIKU, an Inuit-led platform, lets each user set privacy, sharing, stewardship and ownership separately on every post, and it says seeing a post does not grant the right to reuse it.
  - Implication: This supports giving each attribute and edge its own visibility setting rather than one setting per entity. It also suggests a separate 'can see' versus 'can use or re-share' flag on edges, which Trusted/Network visibility circles alone do not express.
  - Source: SIKU Terms of Use (last updated April 13, 2026) (2026-04-13). <https://siku.org/terms>
- **R2-F2** - load-bearing. In the LEO Network, coordinators route each local environmental observation to topic experts who respond, and regional hub organizations help with translation, training and referrals.
  - Implication: Need-to-solution routing works best with a human broker in the loop. A curator or bridge role that receives a need and chooses who to connect is a proven pattern; fully automatic matching is not.
  - Source: Environmental observation, social media, and One Health action: A description of the Local Environmental Observer (LEO) Network (One Health, vol. 6) (2018-10-11). <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6205347>
- **R2-F3** - load-bearing. The Indigenous Peoples Burning Network works through one or two primary contacts per tribe, pueblo or Native NGO, virtual working groups, and an annual in-person gathering hosted by a member Nation, yet it reaches only 12-26 of more than 500 US Tribes.
  - Implication: Nation-level bridges are in practice one or two named people. The graph should model a consented 'primary contact for Nation X in network Y' edge as a key bridge, and should show where coverage is missing (Tribes with no bridge).
  - Source: Igniting Change Over 10 Years of the Indigenous Peoples Burning Network (FAC Learning Network) (2025-04-10). <https://fireadaptednetwork.org/10-years-of-the-indigenous-peoples-burning-network/>
- **R2-F4** - load-bearing, *(corrected)*. The CTUIR Department of Natural Resources organizes its mission around First Foods in a set order (water, salmon, deer, roots, huckleberry, then water again) and manages for the ecological processes and the access that produce those foods.
  - Implication: First Foods are a working, Tribe-authored way to treat foods as first-class nodes with reciprocal care relationships to people and places. A group template could add First Food or species entity kinds, linked by 'cares-for' and 'provides' edges.
  - Source: Director of CTUIR's Department of Natural Resources discusses First Foods framework (NW Power and Conservation Council) (2026-05-28). <https://www.nwcouncil.org/news/2026/05/28/director-of-ctuir-dnr-shares-first-foods-framework-at-may-council-meeting/>
- **R2-F5** - load-bearing. A 2024 federal evaluation found the Indigenous Guardians initiative helped groups connect and share tools, but ECCC's own tracking systems do not talk to each other and results reporting is limited.
  - Implication: The gap is between systems, not within any one of them. Being able to report the network across funder and Nation silos without centralizing data is a real, documented need that the op-log and sync design can meet.
  - Source: Evaluation of Environment and Climate Change Canada's Indigenous Guardians Initiative (2024-08). <https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/corporate/transparency/priorities-management/evaluations/evaluation-eccc-indigenous-guardians-initiative-2024.html>
- **R2-F6**. A 2024 data roundtable of Indigenous Guardians programs named data-sharing agreements or MOUs as the practical way to put data sovereignty into effect, and asked for more training and long-term funding.
  - Implication: Cross-graph bridges should carry a machine-readable agreement (scope, purpose, expiry, revocation) as data on the bridge edge, matching the MOU practice networks already use.
  - Source: ELOKA co-hosts Indigenous Guardians Data Roundtable, Northwest Territories (2024-03-04). <https://eloka.nsidc.org/our-work/stories-events/eloka-co-hosts-indigenous-data-guardians-data-roundtable-northwest-territories-canada>
- **R2-F7**. NWIFC and Washington's state fish agency (WDFW) spent two years merging their fish distribution data into one public map, SWIFD; tribal and state biologists can add records and outside biologists submit through WDFW.
  - Implication: Co-management already produces deliberately public, jointly authored datasets. A T0 bridge cluster that publishes a merged shared reference is a familiar precedent in Tribal practice.
  - Source: Tribal and state co-managers integrate fish distribution data (NWIFC) (2014-04-03). <https://nwifc.org/tribal-state-co-managers-integrate-fish-distribution-data/>
- **R2-F8**. At Tribal Climate Camp, Tribal delegations present projects to each other and share climate plans openly, yet participants report thin staffing and find federal funding overwhelming to navigate.
  - Implication: Reusable artifacts (plans, templates) offered between Nations are a natural graph object. 'Offered-to' edges from a Tribe's plan to peer Tribes could make the camp's culture of gifting persist after the camp ends.
  - Source: Native nations on front lines of climate change share knowledge and find support at intensive camps (AP via Alaska Public Media) (2023-08-30). <https://alaskapublic.org/news/2023-08-30/native-nations-on-front-lines-of-climate-change-share-knowledge-and-find-support-at-intensive-camps>
- **R2-F9**. Seed rematriation runs on trust and deliberate withholding: the seed keeper declines to name varieties to protect recipient Nations, and some communities refuse genetic analysis.
  - Implication: Even inside a broadly shared network, some attributes (variety identity, location) must stay private while the relationship itself is visible. The graph should allow an edge to be public while its payload is redacted.
  - Source: Indigenous farmers are 'rematriating' centuries-old seeds to plant a movement (Popular Science) (2022-02-10). <https://www.popsci.com/environment/indigenous-farmers-rematriation/>
- **R2-F10**. The Navajo and Hopi Families COVID-19 Relief Fund grew from a GoFundMe into 300+ volunteers, 32 staff and 30 distribution teams that delivered 10,000-20,000 care packages a week, against stated red tape and an imminent funding shortfall.
  - Implication: In a crisis, a relationship graph that can be activated quickly (who is a team lead in which chapter, who can drive or sew) matters more than analysis. This suggests an emergency mode that temporarily widens consented visibility and then expires.
  - Source: An incredible team effort: Navajo-Hopi relief group raises $18 million in one year (Navajo Times) (2021-03-18). <https://navajotimes.com/ae/community/an-incredible-team-effort-navajo-hopi-relief-group-raises-18-million-in-one-year/>
- **R2-F11**. Yellowstone bison pass through the Fort Peck Tribes to the InterTribal Buffalo Council, which redistributes them to member Tribes; one 2020 transfer reached 16 Tribes, and Fort Peck speakers frame the buffalo as sacred beings and ancestors.
  - Implication: This is a working multi-hop route for a living relative moving between Nations. The graph could model non-human relatives moving along consented Nation-to-Nation edges, with an intermediary acting as the bridge.
  - Source: ITBC Bison Conservation Transfer Program page (2019-2020 (transfers described; page undated)). <https://www.itbcbuffalonation.org/copy-of-bison-surplus-program>
- **R2-F12**. The 2016 update of Washington's tribal and local health jurisdiction mutual aid agreement, led by the American Indian Health Commission, sets out legal terms for sharing resources in public health emergencies; one regional agreement links three counties and seven Tribes.
  - Implication: Pre-signed agreements that switch on during an emergency already exist between governments. The graph could store such agreements as dormant edges that become active on a declared event.
  - Source: Collaboration Examples - Tribes Toolkit (Northwest Center for Public Health Practice) (2016 (update described; page undated)). <https://www.nwcphp.org/docs/tribes-toolkit/tribal/example.html>

### Exemplars

- **SIKU (Arctic Eider Society).** An Inuit-led social network and mapping app (45,000+ Inuit, First Nations and Metis users) for sea-ice, wildlife and weather observations, with profiles in local dialect and offline GPS trip tracking; used by Guardians programs.
  - Mechanism: Ownership, privacy and stewardship are set on each post. Users can assign 'Indigenous Stewardship' to their content, Indigenous place names stay the property of the organizations that collected them, and seeing a post does not grant the right to reuse it.
  - Lesson: Per-item consent with a separate 'can use' right works at large scale in Indigenous communities. Species and ice terms in Indigenous languages make the land itself a set of first-class entities.
  - <https://siku.org/terms>
- **LEO Network (ANTHC).** An Alaska-born network of local observers and topic experts that has posted unusual environmental events since 2012; it had 1,870 members in 35 countries by 2017.
  - Mechanism: Each post is screened by coordinators, routed to chosen experts who respond, and linked to research. Regional hubs help with translation and referrals, and private messaging is available. Observations have led to food-safety advisories and wildfire smoke tracking.
  - Lesson: Human-brokered routing turns one observation into action. Model the broker or curator role explicitly and record the response loop as a graph edge.
  - <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6205347>
- **Indigenous Peoples Burning Network.** An intertribal cultural-burning network started by the Yurok, Hupa and Karuk Tribes, with The Nature Conservancy (TNC) staff in support; it has grown to roughly 12-26 Tribes.
  - Mechanism: Indigenous co-leads and advisors, one or two primary contacts per Nation, virtual working groups, an annual gathering hosted by a member, and training exchanges (TREX).
  - Lesson: Nation-level bridges are a few named people, and in-person gatherings hold the network together. A graph that showed where bridge contacts are missing could help it reach more of the 500+ Tribes.
  - <https://fireadaptednetwork.org/10-years-of-the-indigenous-peoples-burning-network/>
- **CTUIR First Foods approach.** The Umatilla Tribes' Department of Natural Resources mission is built on the First Foods order: water, salmon, deer, roots, huckleberry.
  - Mechanism: It manages for the ecological processes behind each food and for community access to them, combining traditional ecological knowledge with science to set population and habitat goals; it is grounded in reciprocity from creation beliefs.
  - Lesson: This is a Tribally authored frame that treats non-human beings as kin with obligations both ways. Ship it as an optional group template with food and species nodes and reciprocal care edges, under CTUIR's own terms.
  - <https://www.nwcouncil.org/news/2026/05/28/director-of-ctuir-dnr-shares-first-foods-framework-at-may-council-meeting/>
- **InterTribal Buffalo Council transfer network.** Yellowstone bison move to the Fort Peck Tribes, which pass some to ITBC for redistribution to member Tribes; one 2020 transfer reached 16 Tribes.
  - Mechanism: Multi-hop transfers between Nations through an intertribal intermediary and NGO partners; buffalo are described as sacred beings and ancestors.
  - Lesson: Relatives that move between Nations are a real coordination object. The graph can route living beings, not only people and information.
  - <https://www.itbcbuffalonation.org/copy-of-bison-surplus-program>
- **Indigenous Seed Keepers Network rematriation.** A national network, part of the Native American Food Sovereignty Alliance (NAFSA), that links regional seed projects and returns seeds held in institutions to their communities of origin.
  - Mechanism: Partner growers multiply seed for return, communities are consulted and some refuse genetic analysis, variety names are withheld to protect recipient Nations, and the work proceeds 'at the rate of trust'.
  - Lesson: A relationship can be visible while its contents stay private. Support redacted edge payloads and slow, trust-gated disclosure.
  - <https://www.popsci.com/environment/indigenous-farmers-rematriation/>
- **Navajo and Hopi Families COVID-19 Relief Fund.** A grassroots relief effort that grew from a GoFundMe to more than $18M in a year, serving chapters and villages across both Nations.
  - Mechanism: 300+ volunteers, 30 distribution teams, chapter-level team leads running intake, food and PPE crews, and partnerships with local relief groups and council delegates.
  - Lesson: Crisis coordination needs a fast-activation view of who can do what, and where. An emergency profile that temporarily widens consented visibility, then expires, would serve this need.
  - <https://navajotimes.com/ae/community/an-incredible-team-effort-navajo-hopi-relief-group-raises-18-million-in-one-year/>
- **Tribal Climate Camp (ATNI and partners).** A week-long intensive where Tribal delegations work on their climate plans and present them to the cohort; at least 28 Tribes and intertribal organizations attended in 2023.
  - Mechanism: Team-based cohorts, site visits, expert sessions and peer presentations; plans are openly gifted between Nations, and follow-on effects include new climate staff hires.
  - Lesson: The relationships made at camp fade without something to carry them. A consented record of cohort ties and the plans each Tribe offers would let the network outlast the event.
  - <https://alaskapublic.org/news/2023-08-30/native-nations-on-front-lines-of-climate-change-share-knowledge-and-find-support-at-intensive-camps>

## R3 - Relational frameworks

Across these traditions, the shared design lesson is that a tie between two entities is a responsibility, not just an edge with a visibility level. A connection carries obligations to give back. Trust builds slowly, at the pace of kinship time. Refusal is a legitimate answer. Every record has a whakapapa (genealogy) and stays under guardianship rather than ownership. Whyte's collective continuance and the Tribal Adaptation Menu both value redundancy, so having many ways to meet a responsibility counts as adaptive capacity. That suggests a new product: audits that flag single points of relational failure. GIDA's communique says each Nation's own protocols take priority, and communities without their own may look to CARE and the CARE Data Maturity Model (see Corrections, R3-F10). So bridges between graphs have to carry the protocols of the Nation they come from. The sources caution against generalizing: the IP AI paper, the Menu (Ojibwe and Menominee) and Salmón (Rarámuri iwígara) each speak from a specific Nation or knowledge system. Primary texts were fetched for the load-bearing findings. Salmón's 2000 abstract and Kimmerer's Serviceberry book were seen only through snippets and interviews.

### Findings

- **R3-F1** - load-bearing. Kyle Powys Whyte (Citizen Potawatomi) defines being in a relationship as holding reciprocal, not necessarily equal, responsibilities. He names trustworthiness and ecological redundancy (many repeatable chances to carry out a responsibility, many knowledge holders) as the qualities behind collective continuance, a society's overall adaptive capacity.
  - Implication: Edge kinds in the graph should carry typed, asymmetric responsibilities, not only visibility circles. A redundancy query (needs or responsibilities that rest on one person, one knowledge holder or one bridge) becomes a first-class collective-continuance product, ahead of centrality rankings.
  - Source: Whyte, 'Food Sovereignty, Justice, and Indigenous Peoples: An Essay on Settler Colonialism and Collective Continuance' (Oxford Handbook of Food Ethics) (2018). <https://siue.edu/iks/pdf/FoodSovereingty_and_Justice.pdf>
- **R3-F2**. Whyte argues that a relational tipping point has already been passed before the ecological one: the kin-like qualities of consent, trust, accountability and reciprocity are missing between Tribes and governments, NGOs and corporations, and they cannot be rebuilt quickly.
  - Implication: Trust should accrue only through time and repeated reciprocal acts, never through instant friend requests. Cross-institution bridges could begin at a low circle and widen as reciprocity is shown, and the graph can show where relational repair is owed (for example agency-Tribe ties with one-sided flows).
  - Source: Brooker, 'To solve the climate crisis, we must first repair our relationships' (Grist interview with Kyle Whyte); see also Whyte 2020, 'Too late for indigenous climate justice: Ecological and relational tipping points', WIREs Climate Change 11:e603 (abstract names 'consent, trust, accountability, and reciprocity') (2021-09-08). <https://grist.org/climate/to-solve-the-climate-crisis-we-must-first-repair-our-relationships>
- **R3-F3**. Shawn Wilson (Opaskwayak Cree) frames Indigenous research as a ceremony of staying accountable to relationships, which constrains the choice of topics, methods, analysis and presentation.
  - Implication: Analytics are relational acts. Each query, path search or export that touches an entity should leave a receipt the entity can see (who looked, why, and what came of it), so the analyst stays accountable to the people analyzed.
  - Source: Wilson, Research Is Ceremony: Indigenous Research Methods (Fernwood) - publisher description (2008). <https://fernwoodpublishing.ca/book/research-is-ceremony-shawn-wilson>
- **R3-F4**. Enrique Salmón's kincentric ecology, built from the Rarámuri concept iwígara, holds that people and the rest of the living world form one extended family, and that life in a place is viable only when humans treat it as kin.
  - Implication: Rivers, salmon runs, foods and places should be first-class entities with their own relationships and guardians, not just geographic attributes on people. This should be framed as Rarámuri teaching and one template option, not a universal schema.
  - Source: Paulson, 'Our extended family: plants, animals and soil' (WPR / To The Best Of Our Knowledge, Kinship series), with Salmón 2000, 'Kincentric ecology', Ecological Applications 10(5):1327-1332 (abstract seen only as a snippet) (2022-06-12). <https://www.wpr.org/environment/our-extended-family-plants-animals-and-soil>
- **R3-F5** - load-bearing. Robin Wall Kimmerer (Citizen Potawatomi) describes the Honorable Harvest as never taking the first, taking only what you need, asking permission and actually listening for the answer, and always giving back.
  - Implication: Introduction and data requests must allow a no that is final, carries no penalty and is not re-asked endlessly. Requests should be scoped to need, and a fulfilled request should prompt a reciprocal return (thanks, results, credit) recorded in the op log.
  - Source: Kimmerer, 'Practical Reverence' (Emergence Magazine interview) (2024-11-21). <https://emergencemagazine.org/interview/practical-reverence/>
- **R3-F6**. Two-Eyed Seeing (Etuaptmumk), as given by Mi'kmaw Elder Albert Marshall, means using Indigenous and mainstream knowledges side by side without merging them. Reid et al. note it carries an action imperative: knowledge transforms the holder, who must then act on it.
  - Implication: Indigenous-knowledge and Western evidence about the same need can be shown as two parallel lenses with their own provenance, never fused into one score. A discovered need-to-solution path should create a follow-up obligation for the viewer instead of ending as a viewed result.
  - Source: Reid, Eckert, Lane, Young, Hinch, Darimont, Cooke, Ban & Marshall, 'Two-Eyed Seeing: An Indigenous framework to transform fisheries research and management', Fish and Fisheries 22:243-261 (2020-10-19 (online); 2021 (issue)). <https://www.cclmportal.ca/sites/default/files/2024-02/Fish%20and%20Fisheries%20-%202020%20-%20Reid%20-%20Two%E2%80%90Eyed%20Seeing%20%20%20An%20Indigenous%20framework%20to%20transform%20fisheries%20research%20and%20management.pdf>
- **R3-F7** - load-bearing. Te Mana Raraunga's Māori Data Sovereignty Principles say all data has a whakapapa, and they pair it with whanaungatanga accountabilities to the communities data come from, a 'Connect' sub-principle supporting links between Māori and other Indigenous peoples, and Māori authority to decide which data are tapu (controlled) or noa (open).
  - Implication: The IEEE 2890 provenance envelope should be extended to record purpose, collection context and the parties involved, and should travel with any attribute that crosses a bridge. The tapu/noa decision belongs to the source collective, so a broadly sharing bridge cluster cannot reclassify what it relays.
  - Source: Te Mana Raraunga, Principles of Māori Data Sovereignty, Brief #1 (2018-10). <https://www.temanararaunga.maori.nz/principles-of-maori-data-sovereignty>
- **R3-F8**. Te Hiku Media's Kaitiakitanga License casts the data holder as kaitiaki (guardian, protector, custodian) rather than owner. It requires permission to access or use the material, binds derived works to the license, bars commercial use without a grant, and calls plain open-sourcing a route to digital colonisation.
  - Implication: Broadly shared bridge data should travel under a custodial license that follows derived products, such as network maps, briefs or model training sets, rather than an open license. The license is Te Hiku's own and needs their permission to adapt, so it is a pattern to learn from, not text to reuse.
  - Source: Te Hiku Media, Kaitiakitanga License (LICENSE.md, GitHub) (undated living license (accessed 2026-11-11)). <https://github.com/TeHikuMedia/Kaitiakitanga-License/blob/tumu/LICENSE.md>
- **R3-F9** - load-bearing. The Indigenous Protocol and AI Position Paper sets out seven guidelines. Guideline 2, Relationality and Reciprocity, makes encoding human and non-human relationships a primary design goal and places AI systems inside the circle of relationships under each community's protocols. Guideline 7 says communities decide when to protect and when to share their data.
  - Implication: An AI matchmaker or bridge agent should be an entity in the graph with its own declared relationships, protocols and accountability, admitted by each community's protocol. It should not be an invisible service with unfiltered read access.
  - Source: Lewis (ed.), Indigenous Protocol and Artificial Intelligence Position Paper (Initiative for Indigenous Futures and CIFAR) (2020). <https://spectrum.library.concordia.ca/id/eprint/986506/7/Indigenous_Protocol_and_AI_2020.pdf>
- **R3-F10** - load-bearing, *(corrected)*. The Global Indigenous Data Alliance communique 'CARE Directs Us Home' says locally designed Indigenous data governance principles take priority. Where none exist, communities may look to CARE and the CARE Data Maturity Model, and CARE applies to new data relationships and to previously collected data alike.
  - Implication: Each Nation's data governance protocol should be expressed as data in its group template, with CARE as the default floor. A cross-graph bridge must apply the stricter of the two Nations' protocols, and the rules apply retroactively to data already in the op log, not just to new data.
  - Source: Taitingfong, Carroll, Kukutai et al., 'CARE Directs Us Home: Prioritizing Indigenous Peoples' Community Standards Communiqué' (GIDA), doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.29294120.v1 (2024 (citation); file dated 2025-07; NNI coverage 2025-08). <https://fnigc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/GIDA-Communique-CARE-Directs-Us-Home-Prioritizing-Indigenous-Peoples-Community-Standards-final.pdf>
- **R3-F11**. The AIHEC Indigenous Evaluation Framework rests on four core values: being a people of a place, recognizing our gifts, honouring family and community, and respecting sovereignty. Under it, evaluation belongs to the tribe and is judged locally rather than by how well a program generalizes.
  - Implication: The skills attribute could be modeled as gifts, each paired with a responsibility to the community, and every person and organization anchored to place. Metrics on network health should be defined and owned by each Nation, not a global benchmark.
  - Source: LaFrance & Nichols, 'Reframing Evaluation: Defining an Indigenous Evaluation Framework', Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation 23(2) (basis of AIHEC's 'Telling Our Story in Our Place and Time') (2008 (article); framework book 2009, reissued 2019). <https://nnlm.gov/sites/default/files/2021-08/indigenous%20eval.pdf>
- **R3-F12**. The Tribal Climate Adaptation Menu (Ojibwe and Menominee, by GLIFWC and partners) puts its relationship strategies first on purpose. Its first strategy is to consider cultural practices and seek spiritual guidance, and it warns that the urgency of climate work clashes with the long time relationships with knowledge holders need.
  - Implication: Stories and need routes should be able to carry seasonal and ceremonial timing (for example, winter-only knowledge) and a relationship-before-request order. The Menu's tiered strategies could become a typed vocabulary for needs and actions, adapted by each Nation rather than imposed.
  - Source: Tribal Adaptation Menu Team, Dibaginjigaadeg Anishinaabe Ezhitwaad: A Tribal Climate Adaptation Menu (GLIFWC, Odanah WI), doi:10.2737/NRS-GTR-87-2 (2019 (v1); v2.0 2020-11). <https://www.fs.usda.gov/nrs/pubs/jrnl/2019/nrs_2019_ziigwanikwe_001.pdf>

### Exemplars

- **Te Hiku Media (Te Reo Irirangi o Te Hiku o Te Ika) and the Kaitiakitanga License.** An iwi radio station and Māori-language technology group that built its own speech data and models and licenses code and data under a guardianship license instead of an open one.
  - Mechanism: Access is granted case by case under tikanga, derived works stay bound by the license, commercial use needs an explicit grant, and the license is maintained as a living document.
  - Lesson: Bridging and broad sharing can still be custodial: a license that follows derived products lets a cluster share widely without giving up guardianship.
  - <https://github.com/TeHikuMedia/Kaitiakitanga-License>
- **Abundant Intelligences.** A six-year research and research-creation program (2023-03-01 to 2029-02-28), co-directed by Jason Edward Lewis (Concordia) and Hēmi Whaanga (Massey), that builds AI grounded in Indigenous knowledge systems.
  - Mechanism: A multi-funder partnership (SSHRC, NFRF, MacArthur and others) that develops culturally grounded AI with communities and follows on from the 2020 IP AI position paper.
  - Lesson: A precedent and possible partner for treating an AI matchmaker as a relative under protocol, rather than a neutral tool, inside Community Navigator.
  - <https://www.indigenous-ai.net/abundant/>
- **CARE Data Maturity Model (Collaboratory for Indigenous Data Governance).** The maturity model GIDA's 2024 communique points to as local guidance where a community has no data governance principles of its own, alongside a maintained list of national protocols such as OCAP, Te Mana Raraunga, SODA and Maiam nayri Wingara.
  - Mechanism: Organizations rate their practice against CARE in stages, and community protocols are linked as the governing layer.
  - Lesson: Group templates could reference a Nation's own protocol, or fall back to the CARE floor, and show a per-graph maturity readout rather than a single pass or fail.
  - <https://indigenousdatalab.org/care-data-maturity-model>
- **Little River Band of Odawa Indians sturgeon recovery (as described by Whyte in Daedalus, 2018).** A Tribal sturgeon restoration program whose research design was set by a Cultural Context Committee of elders, scientists and Tribal members, with yearly public release ceremonies.
  - Mechanism: Community trust in and consent to the research design, methods and outcomes, secured through a mixed committee and public ceremony, with feasts that rebuild relationships with settler neighbors.
  - Lesson: Consent can be a witnessed collective event, not just a checkbox. A bridge or broad-sharing grant could require a recorded collective act, matching the project's committee-approval gate.
  - <https://www.amacad.org/sites/default/files/publication/downloads/014_Whyte-pp136-147.pdf>
- **Dibaginjigaadeg Anishinaabe Ezhitwaad (Tribal Climate Adaptation Menu).** A tiered menu of climate adaptation strategies built on Ojibwe and Menominee language and values, designed for other Nations to adapt with their own language and knowledge.
  - Mechanism: It plugs into the NIACS Adaptation Workbook, puts relationship and reciprocity strategies first, and comes with a Guiding Principles document on tobacco, gifting, recognition and time.
  - Lesson: Proof that a Nation-specific framework can serve as a reusable template without being generalized, the same pattern Community Navigator's group templates use.
  - <https://forestadaptation.org/tribal-climate-adaptation-menu>

## R4 - Network science

The evidence points to a few things that matter. First, a network should match the problem: actors who manage connected parts of an ecosystem need ties to each other (Bodin's fit). Second, the type of tie matters: bonding and bridging ties help people survive a crisis, while linking ties to people in power best predict recovery (Aldrich). Third, the quality of a relationship can be measured, as trust and value (PARTNER). Introductions do create new ties: a 90-minute brokered session raised the chance two researchers co-applied for a grant by 75%, and a mutual contact raised tie formation online by 35%. No study I found shows how long such ties last. Brokers carry real costs and burn out, especially when they act as gatekeepers. For Community Navigator, this suggests five things: edges typed by tie class, a fit-gap metric anchored to places and ecosystems, edge-level trust and value fields, a limit on how much each bridge is asked to carry, and ripple or value stories as the way to evaluate outcomes. The federal Tribal Resilience Liaison network ended in July 2026, which leaves bridge roles in Tribal climate work without funding.

### Findings

- **R4-F1** - load-bearing. Collaborative environmental governance works better when the collaboration network matches the ecosystem, meaning actors who manage interconnected parts of a resource are tied to each other.
  - Implication: Metric and feature: model places and ecosystems (watersheds, salmon runs, fire sheds) as entities, then compute a fit gap: pairs of entities tied to the same or linked ecological units but with no edge between them. Ranking those missing ties gives evidence-based introduction prompts. Projected through cn-perm, this exposes structure rather than personal data.
  - Source: Stockholm University news release on Bodin 2017, Science 357(6352), 'Collaborative environmental governance: Achieving collective action in social-ecological systems' (2017-08-18). <https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/475155>
- **R4-F2**. Bodin's work finds that the best structure depends on the problem: tightly closed triangles help where free-riding is a risk, and a clear coordinator helps for temporary problems.
  - Implication: Caution and feature: there is no universally good network shape. Need-to-solution routing should take a problem type (acute event versus chronic commons) and suggest a hub or coordinator for acute needs and closed triads for shared-resource needs, instead of maximizing density or centrality.
  - Source: Stockholm University news release on Bodin 2017 (Science) (2017-08-18). <https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/475155>
- **R4-F3** - load-bearing. Aldrich's research distinguishes horizontal ties (bonding, bridging), which matter for early survival and return after a disaster, from vertical linking ties to people in power, which best predict community recovery.
  - Implication: Metric: label every edge as bonding (within a group or Nation), bridging (across peer groups) or linking (to an agency, funder or other power-holding institution), and give each viewer a bonding/bridging/linking profile. A Nation with strong bonding ties but no linking edges has a recovery risk the graph can show.
  - Source: FEMA PrepTalks Discussion Guide: Social Capital in Disaster Mitigation and Recovery (Dr. Daniel Aldrich), drawing on Aldrich and Meyer 2015, American Behavioral Scientist 59(2) (undated guide (PrepTalk series, c. 2018); underlying article 2015). <https://fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_preptalks_aldrich_discussion-guide.pdf>
- **R4-F4**. Cash and colleagues found that knowledge systems for sustainability work best when boundary organizations actively manage the boundary between knowledge and action, keeping it credible, salient and legitimate to both sides.
  - Implication: Feature: a bridge entity, an organization that consents to link graphs, should carry declared accountability to each side it connects, recorded with provenance in the op log. Show it as a mediated link, not as merged graphs, so each side's legitimacy stays visible.
  - Source: Cash et al. 2003, 'Knowledge systems for sustainable development', PNAS 100(14):8086-8091 (2003). <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12777623/>
- **R4-F5** - load-bearing. The PARTNER tool measures relationship quality as trust (mission alignment, openness, reliability) and value (resources, power and influence, commitment), and Visible Network Labs reports field-testing the measures.
  - Implication: Metric: add optional, viewer-scoped trust and value attributes to edges, rated by each party about the other, so routing can prefer reliable, mission-aligned paths over merely short ones. Each rating is sensitive and should default to private or trusted visibility.
  - Source: PARTNER Trust and Value Metrics: A Brief (Visible Network Labs) (2021-03-30). <https://visiblenetworklabs.com/2021/03/30/partner-trust-and-value-metrics-a-brief/>
- **R4-F6**. Network Impact and the Center for Evaluation Innovation evaluate networks on three pillars: connectivity, health and results.
  - Implication: Metric: a network health panel should report more than structure (connectivity). It also needs health (participation, trust, capacity) and results (needs met, actions taken). The op log can supply the results pillar by timestamping when a need is matched and resolved.
  - Source: Taylor, Whatley and Coffman 2015, 'Network Evaluation in Practice: Approaches and Applications', The Foundation Review 7(2) (2015). <https://scholarworks.gvsu.edu/tfr/vol7/iss2/5>
- **R4-F7** - load-bearing. A systematic review of 24 studies found that brokerage carries significant costs, and that brokers who become gatekeepers to specialized knowledge or resources can be overwhelmed.
  - Implication: Caution and feature: if a large cluster agrees to act as bridges, routing will concentrate load on a few high-betweenness people. Add bridge-load accounting (open requests routed through each node), an availability or capacity setting the person controls, and routing that spreads load across alternative bridges.
  - Source: Long, Cunningham and Braithwaite 2013, 'Bridges, brokers and boundary spanners in collaborative networks: a systematic review', BMC Health Services Research (2013). <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3648408>
- **R4-F8**. Lee and colleagues found across three studies that brokering by keeping people apart (tertius separans) raises the risk of broker burnout and later abusive behavior, compared with brokering by bringing people together.
  - Implication: Feature: design bridging as introduce-and-step-out (tertius iungens). An introduction creates a direct edge between the two parties, so the bridge does not stay a permanent relay or gatekeeper. Success is measured by the direct ties a bridge creates, not by its betweenness.
  - Source: Lee et al. 2024, 'The Strain of Spanning Structural Holes: How Brokering Leads to Burnout and Abusive Behavior', Organization Science 35(1):177-194 (2024-01). <https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ororsc/v35y2024i1p177-194.html>
- **R4-F9** - load-bearing. In a randomized field experiment, 90-minute structured information-sharing sessions raised the probability that a pair of researchers co-applied for a grant by 75%, which shows that search friction blocks collaboration even among people who work close together.
  - Implication: Feature: brokered, purpose-bound encounters (convention sessions, need-matching rounds tied to a funding call) produce real collaborations. Community Navigator can generate small, permission-scoped introduction sets around a concrete opportunity, for example from the TCR Policy Scanner. Caution: collaborations are rare events and the effect was concentrated within the same field.
  - Source: Boudreau et al. 2017, 'A Field Experiment on Search Costs and the Formation of Scientific Collaborations', Review of Economics and Statistics 99(4):565-576 (2017). <https://ideas.repec.org/a/tpr/restat/v99y2017i4p565-576.html>
- **R4-F10**. A randomized online field experiment found that having an existing tie to a mutual contact raised the rate at which new ties formed by 35%, with larger effects when the person had interacted more with that contact.
  - Implication: Feature: routing an introduction through a mutual, consenting contact, and showing that contact, should raise uptake. Caution: the study measured whether ties formed, not whether they lasted, so durability has to be tracked in the op log and not assumed.
  - Source: Mosleh, Eckles and Rand, 'Tendencies toward triadic closure: Field-experimental evidence' (SocArXiv ys8zw; later PNAS) (2024 (preprint)). <https://ideas.repec.org:443/p/osf/socarx/ys8zw.html>
- **R4-F11**. Ripple Effects Mapping is a participatory evaluation method that combines appreciative inquiry with mind mapping to capture program impacts as stories.
  - Implication: Feature: Community Navigator stories (R7) can be authored as ripple maps that trace what an introduction or edge led to, including unintended effects. This gives a relational, story-based results measure that fits Indigenous evaluation practice better than counting ties.
  - Source: National Ripple Effects Mapping Community of Practice (Purdue Extension) (page modified 2026-02-13 (method field guide 2017)). <https://ag.purdue.edu/rrdc/national-initiatives/rem-cop.html>
- **R4-F12**. The federally supported network of Tribal Community Resilience Liaisons, employed mainly by Tribal organizations, ran from 2014 to July 17, 2026, and USGS now describes the BIA-CASC partnership behind it as no longer active.
  - Implication: Caution and opportunity: the human bridge roles in Tribal climate work just lost their federal structure. A consented, Tribally governed record of who bridged what, and for whom, can keep that relational memory through staffing changes. It must support bridge people, not replace them.
  - Source: Tribal Resilience Liaisons, USGS Climate Adaptation Science Centers (undated page, written after 2026-07-17). <https://www.usgs.gov/ecosystems/climate-adaptation-science-centers/tribal-resilience-liaisons>

### Exemplars

- **PARTNER Platform (Visible Network Labs).** A survey and network-analysis tool used by public-health and community coalitions to map partners and score relationship quality.
  - Mechanism: Each partner rates others on trust (mission alignment, openness, reliability) and value (resources, power and influence, commitment). Scores are aggregated into edge- and node-level metrics with improvement thresholds.
  - Lesson: Relationship quality, not only whether a tie exists, is the metric coalitions act on. Community Navigator can store such ratings as permissioned edge attributes, defaulting to private.
  - <https://visiblenetworklabs.com/2021/03/30/partner-trust-and-value-metrics-a-brief/>
- **Network Weaver (June Holley).** A practice and handbook for cultivating networks by having many members act as weavers who connect people strategically.
  - Mechanism: Weavers close triangles (introduce people who share ground) and move a network from hub-and-spoke control toward many distributed connectors.
  - Lesson: Bridging should be widely distributed and introduce-and-step-out. A product can prompt each person to make one strategic introduction instead of routing everything through a few hubs.
  - <https://networkweaver.com/?p=4685>
- **Ripple Effects Mapping.** A participatory evaluation method from Extension (WSU, University of Idaho, NDSU, University of Minnesota) for capturing the intended and unintended impacts of community programs.
  - Mechanism: A group reflects through appreciative inquiry, then builds a mind map of ripples outward from an initiative, often coded against the Community Capitals Framework.
  - Lesson: Outcomes of connection travel as stories along chains. Community Navigator stories can be ripple maps anchored to edges and ops, giving an evaluation layer that respects how communities tell their own impact.
  - <https://conservancy.umn.edu/handle/11299/190639>
- **Wenger-Trayner value-creation framework.** A framework for assessing the value that communities of practice and social learning spaces create.
  - Mechanism: Value-creation stories trace contributions through cycles: immediate, potential, applied, realized, and transformative or reframing value, with enabling and strategic cycles in later versions.
  - Lesson: An edge's worth lies in what it enabled downstream. Value-creation story templates could be a data-defined story type in the group template system.
  - <https://www.wenger-trayner.com/evaluation-framework/>
- **Tribal Community Resilience Liaisons network (BIA TCR and USGS CASCs).** A network of liaisons, mainly employed by Tribal organizations including in the Northwest, who bridged Tribes, agencies and science centers on adaptation planning (2014 to July 2026).
  - Mechanism: Funded human boundary spanners helped more than 100 Tribal Nations with adaptation plans and vulnerability assessments by brokering resources and science.
  - Lesson: Bridges are people with limited capacity and depend on funding. The tool should make their relationships durable and visible with consent, and show their load, since the federal structure has just ended.
  - <https://www.usgs.gov/ecosystems/climate-adaptation-science-centers/tribal-resilience-liaisons>
- **Harvard Medical School randomized research symposium.** A field experiment that used a grant call to randomize which researchers met in 90-minute structured information-sharing sessions.
  - Mechanism: Lowering search costs through a purpose-bound, facilitated encounter raised pair co-application by 75%.
  - Lesson: Opportunity-anchored matchmaking works. Pairing Community Navigator need-to-solution routing with funding opportunities (TCR Policy Scanner) and convening moments is the evidence-backed product loop.
  - <https://ideas.repec.org/a/tpr/restat/v99y2017i4p565-576.html>

### Open questions

- No study I found tracks whether brokered introductions produce durable ties over years; Community Navigator's op log could measure tie survival directly. Is that worth designing in as a first-class metric?
- Bodin's fit concept needs ecological structure in the graph (watersheds, species, fire sheds). Should GeoBase supply those ecological entities, and how is a fit gap computed across permission projections without leaking hidden ties?
- How should the bonding/bridging/linking classification work when one entity (a Tribal Nation acting as a government) is both a peer and a power-holder, given that linking ties assume a vertical hierarchy that sovereignty reframes?
- PARTNER trust and value metrics were validated in public-health coalitions, not Tribal or Indigenous-led networks. Do they translate, or do relational and kinship-based measures need co-design?
- What bridge-load limit or support mechanism prevents the broker burnout documented by Long 2013 and Lee 2024 when many graphs route through a few consenting bridge entities?
- With the Tribal Resilience Liaison network ended July 2026, who now holds the bridge roles in Northwest Tribal climate work, and would they want the tool as a support?
- Cash et al. and Berkes 2009 (bridging organizations in co-management) were seen only as snippets here; the full texts should be checked before any design leans on their specific mechanisms.

## R5 - Sovereign federation technology

As of November 2026, a Rust-plus-WASM stack for consent-gated federation is mostly ready to use. Iroh 1.0 (transport, compiles to WASM), Biscuit 6.0 (offline-checkable capability tokens with Datalog policies), SD-JWT (RFC 9901) with OpenID4VP 1.0 Final (credentials that reveal only chosen claims), VC 2.0 (W3C Recommendation) and Meadowcap (Final spec) can be built on now. The local-first access-control layers are not ready. Keyhive/Beelay are pre-alpha, unaudited and marked "DO NOT use" in production. p2panda-auth/encryption exist but make no stability claims. AT Protocol Spaces is an alpha with no encryption. Solid's W3C successor is still a Working Draft. The privacy tools are usable but narrow: voprf/OpenMined PSI for matching without exposing lists, OpenDP for noisy aggregate counts across the network, and Semaphore v4 for anonymous membership proofs, whose Rust/WASM path is thin. Recommendation: keep cn-sync's SyncTransport abstraction, prototype a Biscuit-based "bridge consent" token over the op log, and treat Keyhive/Willow as designs to watch. Choosing a network protocol remains a human gate.

### Findings

- **R5-F1** - load-bearing. Ink & Switch's Keyhive (signing, encryption and delegation for local-first data) and its relay Beelay are Rust with a keyhive_wasm TypeScript binding under Apache-2.0, but they are explicitly pre-alpha and unaudited.
  - Implication: Keyhive's design (capability delegation plus BeeKEM group keys over a CRDT) is closest to Community Navigator's delegation model and worth studying, but cn-perm should not depend on it until a security audit and a stable release exist.
  - Source: Keyhive Lab Notebook, entry 04 Opening the Pre-Alpha (and github.com/inkandswitch/keyhive README) (2025-03-10 (notebook entries continue to 2026-10-31)). <https://www.inkandswitch.com/keyhive/notebook/>
- **R5-F2**. Automerge 3 (July 2025) cut memory use by over 10x and keeps the Automerge 2 file format; its Rust crate is automerge 0.12.0 (MIT, updated 2026-09-16).
  - Implication: Automerge is a mature CRDT for co-edited documents (such as shared stories or group profiles), but it has no access control of its own, so it would sit beside cn-store's op log and cn-perm, not replace them.
  - Source: Automerge 3.0 blog post; crates.io automerge (2025-07; crate 2026-09-16). <https://automerge.org/blog/automerge-3/>
- **R5-F3** - load-bearing. Meadowcap, Willow's capability system, reached Final status in November 2025 and has a Rust reference implementation (crate willow25 0.5.0 stable, MIT/Apache-2.0, now hosted on Codeberg); it supports owned and communal namespaces and delegation restricted by subspace, path and time.
  - Implication: Meadowcap's split maps onto the project's own model: a Tribe or committee graph as an owned namespace, and each individual as the owner of their own subspace in a communal one, with bridge consent expressed as a delegated read capability limited by path and time.
  - Source: Meadowcap specification (Willow protocol) (Status Final as of 2025-11-21). <https://willowprotocol.org/specs/meadowcap/index.html>

  - Source: p2panda home page (posts 2025-02-24 to 2026-09-16). <https://p2panda.org/>
- **R5-F5** - load-bearing. Iroh 1.0 (2026-06-15; crate iroh 1.4.1, MIT OR Apache-2.0) promises a stable wire protocol and API, and is continuously checked to compile to WASM and run in the browser.
  - Implication: Iroh is the most production-ready candidate for a future cn-sync network adapter that connects devices by public key, but browsers will likely depend on relays (the post states no browser limits), and adopting it is a human-gated network-protocol choice.
  - Source: Iroh 1.0 - Dial Keys, not IPs (2026-06-15). <https://iroh.computer/blog/v1>
- **R5-F6**. AT Protocol's private-data extension, Atproto Spaces, opened as an alpha on 2026-08-20; access is gated by a space authority's membership list, and the data is not encrypted.
  - Implication: Spaces is unsuitable for Tier 2-Tier 3 (T2-T3) sovereign data because the host operator can read every record, but an AT Protocol identity could serve as an optional public-tier (T0) bridge identifier.
  - Source: The Atproto Spaces Alpha is Live (2026-08-20). <https://atproto.com/blog/atproto-spaces-alpha>
- **R5-F7**. Solid has a large real deployment (Flanders, via Inrupt's Enterprise Solid Server), but its W3C standardization successor, Linked Web Storage 1.0, was still a Working Draft in mid-2026, behind its charter dates.
  - Implication: Solid shows that citizen-controlled, per-organization consent works at scale, but it is server-hosted rather than local-first, and the spec is moving, so borrow its consent patterns rather than its protocol.
  - Source: Inrupt case study: Flanders strengthens trusted data economy (undated case study; LWS WD 2026-06-18). <https://www.inrupt.com/case-study/flanders-strengthens-trusted-data-economy>
- **R5-F8**. W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 became a Recommendation on 2025-05-15, and DID 1.1 is a Candidate Recommendation (2026-03-05); BBS signatures, which allow unlinkable selective disclosure, remain an IRTF draft (draft-irtf-cfrg-bbs-signatures-14, 2026-10-29).
  - Implication: Signed attestations such as 'X is a member of Tribe Y's climate office' or 'X agreed to bridge to network Z' can use the standard VC 2.0 envelope now; BBS-based unlinkable presentations should be marked research until the draft becomes an RFC.
  - Source: Verifiable Credentials Data Model v2.0 (2025-05-15). <https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model-2.0/>
- **R5-F9** - load-bearing. SD-JWT, the selective-disclosure format for JSON Web Tokens, is now RFC 9901 (Standards Track, November 2025); OpenID4VP 1.0 is Final (2025-07-09); and SD-JWT VC is in IESG evaluation (draft-26, 2026-11-06).
  - Implication: A person could disclose only chosen attributes (for example a skill and a region but not a name) to another graph using standards that wallets will support; this maps directly onto per-attribute visibility circles and is production-ready for a JWT-based bridge credential.
  - Source: OpenID for Verifiable Presentations 1.0 (and RFC 9901 Selective Disclosure for JSON Web Tokens) (2025-07-09; RFC 9901 2025-11). <https://openid.net/specs/openid-4-verifiable-presentations-1_0.html>
- **R5-F10**. OpenMLS (crate openmls 0.9.2, MIT, updated 2026-10-22) implements MLS (RFC 9420), but its book classes wasm32-unknown-unknown as unsupported (built on CI but not tested) and makes no audit or production claims.
  - Implication: MLS fits end-to-end encrypted Trusted-circle channels, but it assumes a delivery service that orders messages, which clashes with offline concurrent edits; that is why Keyhive built BeeKEM, so treat MLS as an option for a messaging feature, not for the graph store.
  - Source: The OpenMLS Book (crate 2026-10-22). <https://book.openmls.tech/>
- **R5-F11** - load-bearing. Biscuit authorization tokens (crate biscuit-auth 6.0.0 stable, released 2025-07-16, Apache-2.0, about 12.8M downloads; 7.0.0-alpha.1 appeared 2026-11-11) are mature, offline-verifiable capability tokens; UCAN's spec is at 1.0.0, but its Rust crate ucan was last updated in 2023 (0.4.0 stable).
  - Implication: Biscuit's attenuable tokens with Datalog policies could encode 'this entity authorizes itself as a bridge for graph G, attributes A, until date T' as a portable, revocable grant that cn-perm checks before projecting; it is the most production-ready Rust choice, whereas UCAN's Rust support looks stale.
  - Source: crates.io biscuit-auth versions API (2025-07-16). <https://crates.io/api/v1/crates/biscuit-auth/versions>

Privacy-preserving computation is available but narrow: voprf (0.5.0 stable, MIT, updated 2026-11-05) and OpenMined PSI (ECDH with compressed sets, Apache-2.0, has Rust and JS bindings) support private set intersection; OpenDP (0.16.0) is a Rust core that its own README calls 'a work in progress'; and Semaphore v4 is audited, but its circuits are Circom/JS with only thin Rust and WASM paths.
  - Implication: Two graphs can discover shared people, skills or needs without exchanging lists (PSI), publish network-wide aggregate counts with formal privacy guarantees (DP), or let a member prove they belong to a Tribe's group without being identified (Semaphore); PSI is the most feasible near-term primitive for discovering bridges.

## R6 - AI and graph frontier

Between 2024 and 2026, a browser on an ordinary laptop became able to run AI fully offline. WebGPU now ships in all four major browsers. WebLLM keeps up to 80% of native speed. Embedding models under 200MB work without a network. Small multimodal LLMs need 2-3GB of memory. Community Navigator already holds a structured, permission-filtered graph, so it does not need to pay GraphRAG/LightRAG's costly step of having an LLM extract a graph from text. The local-LLM floor that step requires (LightRAG names Qwen3-30B-A3B at about 50 tok/s) is beyond an Iris Xe laptop. The tool can hand an on-device model the viewer's projection directly. Bridging-based ranking (Community Notes, Polis) and connection-oriented cluster matching are graph algorithms that fit cross-institution bridges well. Habermas Machine and Talk to the City show AI drafting group statements, but both used frontier or cloud models. MCP over the projection is useful, but untrusted input plus private data plus an exfiltration path is the main risk. Te Hiku and IP//AI support community-held, benefit-restricted models.

### Findings

- **R6-F1** - load-bearing. WebLLM runs LLM inference entirely inside a web browser on WebGPU plus WebAssembly and keeps up to 80% of native speed on the same device.
  - Implication: An on-device assistant can run inside the existing Vite/WASM app and reason only over the viewer's cn-perm projection. Nothing leaves the machine, and no server is needed, which fits the single-file offline snapshot (R8) in spirit. Model weights (1-4GB) cannot fit the 5MB budget, so they must be a separate cached download.
  - Source: WebLLM: A High-Performance In-Browser LLM Inference Engine (arXiv 2412.15803) (2024-12). <https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15803>
- **R6-F2**. As of November 2025, WebGPU is officially supported in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, although support still varies by browser version and operating system.
  - Implication: In-browser AI is no longer limited to Chrome, so convention or field laptops can run it. Firefox on Linux and Android still lacks WebGPU, so every AI feature needs a WASM-CPU fallback or must degrade cleanly to non-AI graph queries.
  - Source: WebGPU is now supported in major browsers (web.dev) (2025-11-25). <https://web.dev/blog/webgpu-supported-major-browsers>
- **R6-F3**. Transformers.js v3 added WebGPU support that Hugging Face says is up to 100x faster than WASM, with 120 supported architectures and over 1,200 converted models.
  - Implication: Embedding, classification and reranking models can run in the TypeScript layer to give semantic search over attribute text the viewer is allowed to see. The Rust core keeps permission logic, and the model only reads projections.
  - Source: Transformers.js v3: WebGPU Support, New Models & Tasks, and More (Hugging Face) (2024-10-22). <https://huggingface.co/blog/transformersjs-v3>
- **R6-F4** - load-bearing. EmbeddingGemma is a 308M-parameter multilingual embedding model built for on-device and offline use. It runs in under 200MB of RAM when quantized, and its Matryoshka output can shrink from 768 to 128 dimensions.
  - Implication: Matching needs to skills and offers to needs can use local semantic similarity over community-written free text, even on modest laptops. Embeddings of T2/T3 text can be inverted, so they must carry the source attribute's tier and visibility circle and never be exported above that ceiling.
  - Source: Introducing EmbeddingGemma (Google Developers Blog) (2025-09-04). <https://developers.googleblog.com/en/introducing-embeddinggemma/>
- **R6-F5**. Google says Gemma 3n E2B and E4B, multimodal models with 5B and 8B raw parameters, run in as little as 2GB and 3GB of memory.
  - Implication: A 32GB i5 laptop with integrated graphics can comfortably hold a 2-4B-class model to write up path explanations, intake summaries and crosswalk suggestions. Speed was not measured here. Third-party CPU reports for 4B models at Q4 range roughly 7-25 tok/s, which is fine for short outputs and too slow for bulk extraction.
  - Source: Introducing Gemma 3n: The developer guide (Google Developers Blog) (2025-06-26). <https://developers.googleblog.com/en/introducing-gemma-3n-developer-guide/>
- **R6-F6**. GraphRAG uses an LLM to build an entity knowledge graph and then pregenerates community summaries. This approach answers global sensemaking questions over about 1M-token corpora much better than standard RAG.
  - Implication: Community Navigator already has the typed entity graph that GraphRAG pays an LLM to extract, so it can skip that step. Its community detection plus summaries per cluster, computed for each viewer's projection, can produce 'what is this network about' briefs without building a graph from text.
  - Source: From Local to Global: A Graph RAG Approach to Query-Focused Summarization (arXiv 2404.16130) (2024-04-24). <https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130>
- **R6-F7** - load-bearing. The LightRAG project says a locally deployed extraction LLM needs at least Qwen3-30B-A3B and that a model running below about 50 tokens per second may fail to finish a chunk.
  - Implication: Graph extraction with an LLM from text such as CAP documents or engagement notes does not fit an integrated-graphics laptop at that throughput. It belongs in a batch job on hardware the community controls, with reviewed outputs entering the op log as provenance-stamped proposals and never as auto-applied facts.
  - Source: HKUDS/LightRAG README (GitHub) (2026 (README as fetched; paper 2024-10-08)). <https://github.com/HKUDS/LightRAG>
- **R6-F8**. Microsoft reports that LazyGraphRAG has indexing costs identical to vector RAG and 0.1% of full GraphRAG's. It matches GraphRAG global-search quality at more than 700 times lower query cost.
  - Implication: Deferring LLM work to query time and scoping it to the question suits a small local model working on a small projection. The cn-graph traversal does the retrieval, and the LLM only writes the explanation.
  - Source: LazyGraphRAG: Setting a new standard for quality and cost (Microsoft Research) (2024-11-25). <https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazygraphrag-setting-a-new-standard-for-quality-and-cost/>
- **R6-F9** - load-bearing. Exposing tools to an LLM that also sees untrusted input lets attackers drive those tools. Private data, untrusted instructions and an exfiltration vector together form a 'toxic combination'.
  - Implication: An MCP server over Community Navigator must be read-only and serve only cn-perm projections for an authenticated viewer. It must treat intake free text as untrusted, and it cannot share a session with web-fetch or email tools. Otherwise a crafted bio could exfiltrate a bridge entity's broader view.
  - Source: Model Context Protocol has prompt injection security problems (Simon Willison's Weblog) (2025-04-09). <https://simonwillison.net/2025/Apr/9/mcp-prompt-injection/>
- **R6-F10** - load-bearing. In the Birdwatch/Community Notes paper, matrix factorization surfaces notes that 'appeal broadly across heterogeneous user groups'. In a live trial, people who saw these bridging-ranked notes were less likely to reshare the flagged posts.
  - Implication: With group membership as the axis that divides people, Community Navigator can rank needs, priorities and proposed connections by endorsement across different Tribes, agencies or practice networks. Endorsement within a single group would count for less. This yields cross-graph priorities that no single institution can game. A 2026 ICML paper found coordinated raters could push weak items over the threshold, so small-N pilots need minimum-diversity floors.
  - Source: Birdwatch: Crowd Wisdom and Bridging Algorithms can Inform Understanding and Reduce the Spread of Misinformation (arXiv 2210.15723) (2022-10-27). <https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15723>
- **R6-F11**. In the 2024 Habermas Machine study, run with over 5,000 UK participants, group members consistently preferred AI-generated group statements over those written by human mediators, and the AI prominently incorporated dissenting voices.
  - Implication: AI-drafted 'shared statement' loops could help multi-Tribe or committee groups converge on a joint need or funding priority. The study used a fine-tuned frontier-scale model and UK participants, and the human-mediator arm was not clearly told an AI drafted the statements. Tribal use would need a consent-explicit design and a run with a local model, and should not import Western deliberation norms unexamined.
  - Source: AI can help humans find common ground in democratic deliberation (Tessler et al., Science 386:289; research summary PDF) (2024-10-18). <https://www.rivista.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/science.adq2852.pdf>
- **R6-F12**. Te Hiku Media releases its speech models under a Kaitiakitanga license that restricts data use to the benefit of Māori people. A 2026 text-to-speech effort chose a model that runs offline locally and withheld public release.
  - Implication: This is a working precedent for attaching a benefit-restriction license and a TSDF tier to derived AI artifacts such as embeddings, summaries and bridge products, not only to raw records. Each cross-graph product Community Navigator generates should carry a license or use-condition field in its provenance envelope.
  - Source: Indigenous AI voice models (IEEE Spectrum) (2026-05-21). <https://spectrum.ieee.org/indigenous-ai-voice-models-maori>

- **R6-F10**: "snippet: annotations that "appeal broadly across heterogeneous user groups," which they call "bridging-based ranking.""

### Exemplars

- **Community Notes (X).** A crowd fact-annotation system that publishes a note only when raters who usually disagree both find it helpful.
  - Mechanism: Matrix factorization separates a note's intercept (helpfulness regardless of viewpoint) from rater viewpoint factors. The 2022 paper set the publication threshold at an intercept of 0.40, and the algorithm is open source.
  - Lesson: Replace political viewpoint with institutional or Tribal membership, and bridge entities can surface needs and proposals that multiple networks endorse. A minimum-diversity floor guards against coordinated rating in small pilots.
  - <https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15723>
- **Talk to the City (AI Objectives Institute).** An open-source LLM pipeline that turns interviews, free text and Polis output into clustered opinion maps tied to verbatim quotes. Taiwan's Ministry of Digital Affairs uses it and now hosts its own instance.
  - Mechanism: An LLM extracts arguments, clusters and subclusters them, and links each summary back to individual responses to limit hallucination.
  - Lesson: Self-hosting by a government shows a sovereign deployment path. Linking every summary to its source suits provenance (R10). Swapping the frontier LLM for a local one is the open feasibility question.
  - <https://ai.objectives.institute/talk-to-the-city>
- **Habermas Machine (Google DeepMind, Science 2024).** An AI mediator that iteratively drafts group statements from members' opinions and critiques.
  - Mechanism: A fine-tuned LLM with a reward model generates candidate statements, and participants' critiques feed revision rounds that maximize group endorsement while incorporating dissent.
  - Lesson: A critique-and-revise loop over the members of a permission-scoped cluster could draft joint need statements for multi-Tribe proposals. Consent and disclosure must be explicit.
  - <https://www.rivista.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/science.adq2852.pdf>
- **Connection-Oriented Cluster Matching (Gitcoin, from Miller/Weyl/Erichsen).** A quadratic-funding variant used in Gitcoin rounds GG19 and GG20 that gives more matching to projects backed by diverse social clusters.
  - Mechanism: Clusters are inferred from donation patterns or social data. Matching is discounted for donors who look similar and boosted for support that spans clusters.
  - Lesson: The bridge graph itself can serve as the 'social information' for allocating a pooled fund, mutual-aid resources or staff time toward efforts with broad cross-community support. This needs broad sharing of membership edges, which is the sovereignty trade-off to name.
  - <https://radicalxchange.org/media/blog/designing-pluralistic-mechanisms-for-quadratic-funding>
- **Te Hiku Media.** A Māori-run organization that built te reo Māori speech AI, refused to give its data to outside firms, and licenses models for Māori benefit only.
  - Mechanism: The Kaitiakitanga license restricts use to community benefit, runs on its own platform rather than global hosting, and uses offline-capable local models with withheld release.
  - Lesson: Sovereignty extends to derived AI artifacts. Community Navigator should license and tier its embeddings, summaries and bridge products, not only raw records.
  - <https://spectrum.ieee.org/indigenous-ai-voice-models-maori>
- **WebLLM / MLC.** An open-source JavaScript engine that runs quantized LLMs in the browser through WebGPU.
  - Mechanism: Machine-learning compilers (MLC-LLM, Apache TVM) produce WebGPU kernels, and the engine exposes an OpenAI-style API.
  - Lesson: This is the most direct route to an on-device assistant that only ever sees the viewer's projection, with no relay or server involved.
  - <https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15803>

### Open questions

- Actual tokens per second and RAM for a 2-4B Q4 model in WebLLM on Intel Iris Xe (i5-1340P) were not measured. Running llama-bench and the WebLLM demo on the target laptop would settle this.
- No primary source was found for LLM-assisted crosswalks between Indigenous or community-defined taxonomies. The evidence comes from clinical (CDE-Mapper, 2025) and occupational-code (NAACL SRW 2025) crosswalks, both of which keep humans as final authority. Whether small local models reach acceptable precision on short community vocabularies is untested.
- The IP//AI guideline text and the 2025 GIDA communiqué could not be read verbatim: the PDFs were compressed and the page only summarizes. Whether GIDA's February 2026 AI communiqué was finalized is unconfirmed.
- Is there a Tribal or Indigenous deployment of bridging-based ranking or AI-mediated deliberation? None was found.
- How should tier ceilings propagate to derived artifacts (embeddings, cluster summaries, bridge scores) computed over a mix of T1 and T2 inputs? Max-tier-of-inputs is the safe default, but it may make bridge products unshareable.
- Can bridging and cluster-match scores be computed under the permission model, using only edges each bridge entity consented to expose, without a central party seeing the full multi-graph? Secure aggregation or computation done locally at each node in the network is unexplored here.

## R7 - Commons, products and harms

Shared network and directory data has produced real goods in Indigenous and civic settings: tribally held repositories (Native BioData Consortium), community-controlled monitoring data (Indigenous Navigator), provenance labels that stay attached to records (Local Contexts at Manaaki Whenua), federated directories (Murmurations, Open Referral HSDS), and grant coalitions such as the 31-Tribe Bristol Bay award. The worst harm documented was TigerSwan at Standing Rock. The firm built watch lists and dossiers on Indigenous activists, mapped who was linked to whom, and later reused the files against unrelated movements. Small populations face a two-sided problem: privacy noise reduced 2020 Census accuracy for small Tribal areas and population thresholds will cut age-by-sex detail for most tribal groups (see Corrections, R7-F4), while withholding small counts has kept Tribes from data about their own members. Four mitigation patterns held up: data stays private until the community approves publication, consent can be withdrawn later, provenance labels set by the community travel with the data, and data is stored on Tribal land or under Tribal law. For Community Navigator, a bridge entity's edges are the most sensitive data in the graph. A link that consents to be visible as a bridge is not consent to be analyzed for who is central.

### Findings

- **R7-F1** - load-bearing. TigerSwan, the pipeline security firm at Standing Rock, combined online monitoring, flyovers, radio intercepts and paid database searches into watch lists and dossiers on Indigenous activists and environmental groups, and sometimes shared them with law enforcement.
  - Implication: A graph that shows who is connected to which Tribe, organization or campaign is what a hostile party wants for profiling, so a bridge's edge list should be the most protected thing the system holds. Edges should never be exportable in bulk, even when the nodes themselves are public.
  - Source: Grist (with The Intercept), TigerSwan documents: Dakota Access pipeline, Standing Rock surveillance (Brown and Sadasivam) (2023-04-13). <https://grist.org/accountability/tigerswan-documents-dakota-access-pipeline-standing-rock-surveillance/>
- **R7-F2**. After the Standing Rock camps were cleared, TigerSwan pointed the same intelligence work at other movements, including climate organizing and anti-Trump protests, and tracked people across state lines.
  - Implication: Network data collected for one purpose gets reused for others. Each sharing grant should name its purpose and expire, and the op log should be able to prove which projections were issued to whom, not only which data exists.
  - Source: The Intercept, As Standing Rock camps cleared out, TigerSwan expanded surveillance to array of progressive causes (2017-06-21). <https://theintercept.com/2017/06/21/as-standing-rock-camps-cleared-out-tigerswan-expanded-surveillance-to-array-of-progressive-causes/>
- **R7-F3**. Two Zapotec communities said they were not told that a participatory mapping project in Oaxaca was funded through the US Army's Foreign Military Studies Office, and called it geo-piracy.
  - Implication: Participatory mapping can still be extractive when funders and downstream uses are hidden. Any entity that consumes a bridge projection should have to declare its funder and purpose, and that declaration should be visible to the communities whose data it reads.
  - Source: Upside Down World and El Enemigo Común coverage of México Indígena / Bowman Expeditions (advocacy outlets) (2009 (older than the 2019-2026 window; included as the standard example of extractive mapping)). <https://upsidedownworld.org/archives/mexico/military-backed-mapping-project-in-oaxaca-under-fire/>
- **R7-F4** - load-bearing, *(corrected)*. The noise the 2020 Census added for privacy (differential privacy) greatly affected accuracy for small American Indian, Alaska Native and Native Hawaiian areas. Separately, roughly 80 percent of tribal groups that previously received a full suite of demographic data would lose age-by-sex detail; per the Jev check, the source ties that loss to minimum population thresholds for detailed tribal reporting, not to the privacy noise.
  - Implication: In small populations, both re-identification and privacy noise do real harm. Aggregate products should give Tribes their own exact counts and apply suppression or noise only to outside viewers. Who controls that setting is a sovereignty decision, not a technical default.
  - Source: Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis CICD Working Paper 2023-05, New 2020 Census rules make it harder to navigate Native American data (Gregg, Trostle, Liebler) (2023-08-09). <https://minneapolisfed.org/research/cicd-working-paper-series/new-2020-census-rules-make-it-harder-to-navigate-native-american-data>
- **R7-F5**. Tribal public-health guidance says small-number suppression should not be applied to data released to Tribes, because worries about small numbers have been used to deny Tribes data about their own members.
  - Implication: Suppression should depend on who is viewing (TSDF tier plus visibility circle), not be a global rule. A Tribe viewing its own members sees full counts; outsiders get thresholded aggregates.
  - Source: CSTE Tribal epidemiology toolkit, Data Collection (undated (search snippet only)). <https://tribal.cste.org/?page_id=49>
- **R7-F6** - load-bearing. The Indigenous Navigator keeps community submissions private by default and requires free, prior and informed consent for upload, publication and reuse. Communities can make published data private again or take it down.
  - Implication: Community Navigator can copy this lifecycle directly: submit, then review, then a separate publication step, with publication revocable. The 'serve as a bridge' flag should be a revocable publication event in the op log, not a field set once.
  - Source: Indigenous Navigator, Privacy Issues (data governance, privacy and consent page) (page live 2026 (site copyright 2014-2026)). <https://indigenousnavigator.org/node/288>
- **R7-F7**. The Native BioData Consortium, founded in 2018, keeps Indigenous genetic and health data on the Cheyenne River Sioux Reservation under tribal regulation, with a tribal community advisory board.
  - Implication: Storing data under Tribal jurisdiction is a mitigation that has worked. For the graph, this means each Tribe's partition should be able to live on a node that Tribe controls. Bridges should exchange projections, not raw data, which fits the local-only SyncTransport design.
  - Source: Native BioData Consortium, About Us (page current 2025-2026 (founded 2018)). <https://nativebio.org/about-us>
- **R7-F8**. Manaaki Whenua (Landcare Research, New Zealand) used the Local Contexts Hub API to attach Biocultural Notices to a whole open biodiversity database. Iwi then added their own Labels to specimens from their territory.
  - Implication: Two layers work together: the institution applies Notices across a whole collection, and the community chooses its own Labels. This fits the provenance envelope well, and Label references could travel on every edge a bridge exports.
  - Source: Local Contexts, BC Labels and Notice on Manaaki Whenua (2022-10-05 (updated 2023-08-01)). <https://localcontexts.org/bc-labels-and-notice-on-manaaki-whenua/>
- **R7-F9**. GIDA (Global Indigenous Data Alliance) frames the CARE principles as a response to open-data pressure, because a push for data sharing alone conflicts with Indigenous Peoples asserting control over their data.
  - Implication: Even with sharing relaxed, every bridge product should state its collective benefit and who holds authority to control it, so open sharing does not become the default goal.
  - Source: Global Indigenous Data Alliance, The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance (drafted 2018-11-08; overview 2019-10-17; Carroll et al. Data Science Journal 2020-11-04). <https://www.gida-global.org/careprinciples>
- **R7-F10** - load-bearing. Under the Murmurations protocol, entities publish JSON profiles about themselves and submit the URLs to shared indexes, and any number of maps and directories are built from that distributed data.
  - Implication: This is a working model for bridge entities: each publishes a self-hosted public profile and aggregators build views from many of them. However, Murmurations has no access tiers, so it suits only T0 or Public data, and private edges need the cn-perm layer on top.
  - Source: Murmurations Network home page (undated protocol page (blog posts 2023-08-22, 2024-06-04)). <https://murmurations.network/>
- **R7-F11** - load-bearing. NOAA's Climate Resilience Regional Challenge (2024) funded Tribal consortium work, including $2,000,000 to the Bristol Bay Native Association, led by 31 Tribes, to form a Regional Resilience Collaborative, and $74,950,045 to the Alaska Native Tribal Health Consortium, which includes knowledge-sharing networks.
  - Implication: A concrete product with money behind it: from consenting bridge entities, generate a proposal-ready partnership roster with capacity statements and a record of past collaboration. The coalition is the deliverable, and every node in it has opted in.
  - Source: NOAA Office for Coastal Management, Climate Resilience Regional Challenge Funded Projects (awards announced 2024-07). <https://coast.noaa.gov/funding/resilience-challenge/projects.html>
- **R7-F12**. The Ventura River Watershed Council publishes a Kumu map of relationships among stewardship organizations, built only from what survey respondents reported, with no stated data limits or privacy terms.
  - Implication: Typical ecosystem atlases record one-sided claims (A says it works with B) and publish them openly. A two-sided edge, asserted by A and confirmed by B and tiered by both, would be a real advance over Kumu-style atlases.
  - Source: Ventura River Watershed Council, Kumu Network Map (about 2026-04 (from upload path; no date on page)). <https://venturawatershed.org/kumu-map>

### Exemplars

- **Indigenous Navigator (six-partner consortium including the ILO, IWGIA, Tebtebba, AIPP, Forest Peoples Programme and the Danish Institute for Human Rights; EU-funded; since 2014).** Survey tools, data explorers, a rights-implementation index and country portals built from community-generated data on Indigenous rights in 31 countries.
  - Mechanism: Data is private on upload. Consent (FPIC) is required for collection, publication and reuse. Publication is a separate reviewed step, and communities can reverse it later. Commercial reuse requires permission, and attribution goes to the community.
  - Lesson: Treat publication as a revocable event separate from data entry. Also note the naming overlap: 'Indigenous Navigator' is an established Indigenous-data brand, and 'navigator' is also a common human-services job title. Community Navigator should check its name against both before going public.
  - <https://indigenousnavigator.org/node/288>
- **Local Contexts Hub at Manaaki Whenua.** Biocultural Notices on more than 675,000 specimen records in an open biodiversity database, with iwi-applied Labels on specimens from their own territories.
  - Mechanism: The institution applies Notices in bulk through the Hub API. Each community decides on its own Labels, which then appear in the record metadata.
  - Lesson: Provenance and protocol metadata can be attached by machine and stay with the record. Bridge exports should carry Label references rather than free-text terms.
  - <https://localcontexts.org/bc-labels-and-notice-on-manaaki-whenua/>
- **Native BioData Consortium.** A tribally located biorepository and data institute; project leader on the NIH-funded RADx Tribal Data Repository.
  - Mechanism: Physical and legal location on the Cheyenne River Sioux Reservation, governance under tribal regulation, and a tribal community advisory board.
  - Lesson: Storing data under Tribal jurisdiction is a working mitigation. A Tribe should be able to keep its partition in its own store and share only projections.
  - <https://nativebio.org/about-us>
- **Murmurations protocol.** A distributed directory protocol in which many maps and aggregators read the same self-published profiles.
  - Mechanism: Entities host their own JSON profiles, shared indexes track the URLs and update times, and aggregators pull profiles that match a schema.
  - Lesson: A ready pattern for opt-in public bridge profiles. It has no access tiers, so it works only for T0 data.
  - <https://murmurations.network/>
- **Open Referral HSDS (Human Services Data Specification).** A shared data standard that lets separate human-services directories exchange records about organizations, services and locations, with an API suite and record-matching tools.
  - Mechanism: A common schema with local extensions. In federation, each publisher maintains its own feed and an agreed profile governs how feeds are merged.
  - Lesson: Mapping the Organization and Service entities to HSDS would let Community Navigator draw on existing resource directories without re-entering data. Record matching across feeds is where duplicates and errors appear.
  - <https://openreferral.org/>
- **Te Hiku Media Kaitiakitanga License.** A guardianship (stewardship) license on Māori-language data collected by an iwi media organization. Reporting says it bars surveillance, tracking and mining of the data.
  - Mechanism: A license grounded in tikanga Māori that binds outside partners. Te Hiku says it gives priority to requests from Indigenous communities.
  - Lesson: License terms can travel with a dataset and forbid particular uses, such as surveillance. A bridge grant could carry machine-readable prohibited-use terms.
  - <https://tehiku.nz/te-hiku-tech/te-hiku-dev-korero/25141/data-sovereignty-and-the-kaitiakitanga-license>
- **Bristol Bay Regional Resilience Collaborative (NOAA CRRC award to Bristol Bay Native Association).** A climate adaptation planning program led by 31 federally recognized Tribes, funded at $2M in 2024.
  - Mechanism: An existing Tribal consortium stands as the lead applicant. The federal award pays to formalize regional coordination.
  - Lesson: A grant coalition is the most immediate product of a network of bridges. Generating consortium rosters and capacity statements from consenting nodes meets a real, funded need.
  - <https://coast.noaa.gov/funding/resilience-challenge/projects.html>

## R8 - Individual consent UX

The evidence supports one design. Show each person what a specific viewer will see ("audience view"). Lipford 2008 found this improved understanding, and PoPETs 2025 found that every Facebook user studied misjudged their actual settings. Store each consent as a small Nissenbaum-style flow norm (who shares, who receives, about whom, what information, on what condition), not as a bare circle level. Let consent travel with the data across bridges (Kaye's dynamic consent). Give the person a revocable receipt and a log of who accessed what (Kantara, Inrupt access grants, MyData operators). Give the person an ID they keep for life, with affiliations as dated edges that institutions vouch for. Then job changes and moves between Tribal departments keep their relationships (ORCID, AT Protocol). Make erasure real by encrypting each person's data with its own key and destroying the key (crypto-shredding). Its legal status is contested. Tribal sources add a limit: individual consent must sit inside community governance.

### Findings

- **R8-F1** - load-bearing. An interface organised around audiences, showing the profile as each audience sees it, measurably improved people's understanding of their privacy settings compared with a list of settings.
  - Implication: The main sharing screen should not be a table of attributes against circles. It should be 'see my record as' a Trusted peer, a Group member, a Network bridge, or Public, with each view rendered by cn-perm. Controls to hide or show sit on that rendered view.
  - Source: Lipford, Besmer, Watson - Understanding Privacy Settings in Facebook with an Audience View (USENIX UPSEC 2008) (2008 (older than the 2019-2026 window; it is the founding study of this design)). <https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/upsec08/tech/full_papers/lipford/lipford_html/>
- **R8-F2** - load-bearing. In a 2025 study of 195 Facebook users with a browser add-on, every participant had at least one setting that differed from what they expected, usually more permissive than they believed, and their trust dropped once they saw the gap.
  - Implication: Assume people will misjudge their own circles. The tool should state the gap proactively, for example 'You think this is Group-only; 3 network bridges can see it'. This is most urgent when an entity opts in as a broad bridge or when a TSDF tier ceiling silently lowers a visibility choice. Defaults also need testing against what people expect.
  - Source: Lowens, Scarnecchia, Im, Afnan, Chen, Zou, Schaub - Misalignments and Demographic Differences in Expected and Actual Privacy Settings on Facebook (PoPETs 2025) (2025). <https://petsymposium.org/popets/2025/popets-2025-0025.php>
- **R8-F3**. Facebook's own 'View As' preview was the attack surface for its 2018 account-token breach, and the feature was switched off for about eight months.
  - Implication: An audience preview must ask cn-perm for a projection for an imagined viewer. It must never act as that viewer or obtain that viewer's credentials or session. This fits the existing rule that the frontend renders only core projections. The preview path needs its own adversarial tests.
  - Source: Meta Newsroom - Security Update (2018-09-28 (updated 2019-05-14)). <https://about.fb.com/news/2018/09/security-update/>
- **R8-F4** - load-bearing. Dynamic consent is a personalised digital interface. Participants can consent to new projects or change their choices in real time, and their preferences travel with their data when it is shared with third parties.
  - Implication: When a fact crosses a bridge into another graph, its consent should go with it as part of the op (the provenance envelope plus the permission), not stay as a rule held only in the source graph. The receiving graph's cn-perm must enforce it. A later change of mind should then reach the other graph through the op log.
  - Source: Kaye, Whitley, Lund, Morrison, Teare, Melham - Dynamic consent: a patient interface for twenty-first century research networks (Eur J Hum Genet) (2014-05-07 online (volume dated 2015)). <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4130658/>
- **R8-F5**. Indigenous genomics scholars treat dynamic consent as more responsive, but say it must sit inside wider community control over research. Many communities want consent asked again for each use.
  - Implication: Each person controls their own record, but a flow can need two keys: the person's consent and the governing body's authorisation (for example ATNI Climate or a Tribe's data steward), shown as separate checks. 'Ask me each time' should be offered as a sharing condition alongside standing grants.
  - Source: Garrison, Carroll, Hudson - Entwined Processes: Rescripting Consent and Strengthening Governance in Genomics Research with Indigenous Communities (J Law Med Ethics 48(1)) (2020). <https://cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-law-medicine-and-ethics/article/entwined-processes-rescripting-consent-and-strengthening-governance-in-genomics-research-with-indigenous-communities/E722581A3EAEB00EC5F40AD9A223DE75>
- **R8-F6**. The Kantara Consent Receipt (v1.1, 2018, later carried into ISO work) is a standard consent record that both people and machines can read. It records the parties, the purpose, and the categories of personal data.
  - Implication: Every grant a person makes, especially consenting to serve as a bridge, should produce a receipt they keep. It would record who can see what, for what purpose, which TSDF tier, and until when, and hold the op ID that revokes it. That gives the intake form's promises something concrete to point to.
  - Source: Lizar - W3C vocabulary workshop position paper on the Kantara Consent Receipt (2018). <https://www.w3.org/2018/vocabws/papers/lizar.txt>
- **R8-F7**. In Inrupt's Solid server, a request for access states the access mode, the resources, and the purpose. The owner grants or denies it, and the resulting grant is a verifiable credential the owner can revoke, valid between an issue date and an expiry date.
  - Implication: Bridging between graphs can be modelled as request-then-grant ops in cn-store. One graph asks a person or entity to act as a bridge; the person approves a scoped, expiring grant. Revoking it is just another appended op. Expiry by default suits pilot-era bridges.
  - Source: Inrupt Docs - Access Requests and Grants (accessed 2026-11 (ESS 2.x documentation)). <https://docs.inrupt.com/security/authorization/access-requests-grants/>
- **R8-F8**. MyData's operator reference model makes permission management and logging core functions: people see an overview of their data transactions and connections, and operators record who accessed what and when.
  - Implication: A person at the centre of their own graph needs a 'who has seen me' ledger alongside the 'who can see me' preview. It could be built from projection requests recorded in the op log, and is especially needed for anyone acting as a cross-graph bridge.
  - Source: MyData Global - Understanding MyData Operators (white paper) (2020-04). <https://mydata.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Understanding-Mydata-Operators.pdf>
- **R8-F9** - load-bearing. Contextual integrity defines privacy as whether information flows appropriately, specified by five parameters. It also warns that rules covering raw facts may not cover conclusions inferred from them.
  - Implication: A circle level only covers the recipient. A grant should also record the sending graph, the subject, the attribute, and the condition (for example 'with consent', 'reciprocal', 'for drought response only'). Products built across graphs, such as inferred routes from a need to a solution, need their own rule. Nissenbaum notes the crucial question is 'whether privacy norms governing lower-order data are sufficient for the inferred higher-order data'.
  - Source: Nissenbaum - Contextual Integrity Up and Down the Data Food Chain (Theoretical Inquiries in Law 20(1)) (2019). <https://nissenbaum.tech.cornell.edu/papers/Contextual%20Integrity%20Up%20and%20Down.pdf>
- **R8-F10**. In ORCID, a researcher sets visibility item by item (Everyone, Trusted Organizations, Only me), while the persistent iD itself is always public.
  - Implication: Give each person one stable UUIDv7 that they keep for life. Model affiliations (Tribal department, committee, employer) as dated edges the institution vouches for, with visibility set per edge. A job change then ends an edge and adds another; the person's relationships and history stay with them.
  - Source: University of Victoria Libraries - ORCID privacy guide (accessed 2026-11). <https://libguides.uvic.ca/orcid/privacy>
- **R8-F11** - load-bearing. Crypto-shredding encrypts sensitive attributes with a separate key per subject and erases them by deleting the key, leaving the append-only log intact. Its weaknesses are key management, the encryption itself weakening over time, and copies or derived values held by consumers.
  - Implication: This settles the open question in ADR-005 (I:\community-connector\docs\adr\ADR-005-remote-intake.md, lines 1237-1240) about whether removal means no longer shown or true erasure. Encrypt attribute values in cn-store ops with a key per person; tombstoning that key makes 'take me out of the network' true in every fold and export. Search results report that regulators dispute whether deleting a key counts as erasure (Germany is cited).
  - Source: Verraes - Eventsourcing Patterns: Crypto-Shredding (2019-05-13). <https://verraes.net/2019/05/eventsourcing-patterns-throw-away-the-key/>

### Exemplars

- **ORCID.** A persistent researcher identifier and a record the researcher owns, carried across employers for a whole career.
  - Mechanism: The iD is always public. Each item has its own visibility (Everyone, Trusted Organizations, Only me). Organisations the person authorises can add affiliations, and the person reviews who has access.
  - Lesson: An identity owned by the person, with affiliations vouched for by institutions as separate edges, solves moves between Tribal departments and organisations without the person losing their graph.
  - <https://libguides.uvic.ca/orcid/privacy>
- **Inrupt Solid access requests and grants.** A personal data store (pod) where apps and agents must ask before reading the owner's data.
  - Mechanism: A request names the access mode, the resources, and the purpose. The owner approves or denies. The grant is a verifiable credential with an expiry date and can be revoked.
  - Lesson: Bridging between graphs works best as request, grant, receipt and revoke, each stored as an op, rather than one 'I am public' switch.
  - <https://docs.inrupt.com/security/authorization/access-requests-grants/>
- **Local Contexts TK Labels and Notices (with Mukurtu CMS).** Labels set by Indigenous communities that travel with cultural material and state protocols (sacred, seasonal, gender-restricted) and who to ask for permission.
  - Mechanism: Communities set and customise the Labels. Researchers and institutions apply a fixed-wording TK Notice through the Local Contexts Hub, which notifies the relevant community when it is used.
  - Lesson: Community rules can sit on a record next to individual consent, and any outside use can trigger a notice back to the community. That is a model for collective governance of a cross-graph bridge.
  - <https://localcontexts.org/notice/tk-notice/>
- **NCI / NCIG donor-controlled Indigenous genomic data platform (Australia).** An Indigenous-led genomic repository designed so donors control how their data is used.
  - Mechanism: Donor preferences are built into the repository design and encoded in a machine-readable consent model using the Data Use Ontology (DUO).
  - Lesson: Consent should be a typed condition checked by the core at query time, not prose in a form. This parallels putting purpose-scoped conditions into cn-perm.
  - <https://jcsmr.anu.edu.au/news-events/news/towards-long-term-dynamic-consent-indigenous-genomic-research>
- **AT Protocol (Bluesky) account migration.** A social network in which a user can move their account, including follows and followers, between independent data hosts.
  - Mechanism: The identity is a portable identifier (DID) the user controls with rotation keys. Data moves to a new host and the identity record is pointed at it, so the social graph carries over.
  - Lesson: Relationships can outlast the institution hosting them. That bears directly on R5: a person's edges should follow them from one community instance to another, not stay with a facilitator.
  - <https://bsky.network/blog/incoming-migration>
- **MyData operators.** A family of interoperable personal-data operators that organise sharing on a person's behalf.
  - Mechanism: Permission management lets people see and control their data transactions and connections. Logging records who accessed what and when. Operators are meant to be interchangeable.
  - Lesson: Pair a ledger of 'who has accessed me' with the preview of 'who can see me', and make each person's record exportable so they can change host.
  - <https://mydata.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Understanding-Mydata-Operators.pdf>

### Open questions

- Does crypto-shredding (destroying a per-person key) count as erasure under the laws and Tribal codes that apply to ATNI pilot data? Sources say German regulators doubt it. This decides how ADR-005's removal promise is worded (I:\community-connector\docs\adr\ADR-005-remote-intake.md, lines 1237-1240).
- When a person and a Tribe or community steward disagree about an entity acting as a cross-graph bridge, whose condition wins, and does the person keep a unilateral right to withdraw?
- Do audience-preview improvements measured on Facebook-style profiles carry over to a 3D graph, where the visible thing is a neighbourhood of edges rather than a profile page? This needs a small usability test on synthetic data.
- Should conclusions inferred across graphs (for example a discovered route from a need to a solution through bridge entities) carry their own consent rule, or inherit the strictest rule of the edges they cross (Nissenbaum's inference problem)?
- Is a portable, person-held identifier wanted in Tribal settings, or do communities prefer identity vouched for by the Nation? This is a governance question that falls under the identity-standard human gate.
